# Last Call — Murder at Blackwood House

Status: playable MVP under active build.

- 4–8 players.
- Every phone is a private character memory and guide.
- The active investigator taps a person and selects a question.
- The target phone prompts what must be said aloud and what may stay hidden.
- After every full pass around the table, all phones interrupt with new evidence.
- Evidence progression can include public evidence, private clues, suspicious information, and story interruptions.
- Everyone eventually makes a private accusation.
- The phones reveal the murderer and who solved it.

## Locked design rules

### Phone-value rule
The phone must add meaningful gameplay value, not merely digitize something that works just as well without it. The phone is the brain and the guide; the people are the game.

### Minimum-player solvability
Every mystery must be completely solvable at its advertised minimum player count. Last Call supports 4–8 players. The four-player core cast always contains every clue path required to solve the case. Players 5–8 add suspects, secrets, red herrings, relationships, and investigative depth, but never information required for the minimum-player version to be solvable.

### No required note-taking
Players may take notes if they enjoy it, but note-taking is never required. The phone's Case File should remember revealed facts, evidence, private clues, and investigation history. The phone organizes facts; the players make deductions.

### Truthful misdirection
The phone may misdirect players through truthful but incomplete information, suspicious wording, innocent secrets, coincidences, red herrings, private clues, and timed interruptions. It must never present fabricated or objectively false evidence as fact.

Information classes:
- **Evidence** — objectively true facts about the case.
- **Private Clue** — truthful information delivered to only one or a few players.
- **Suspicious Information** — truthful information that appears incriminating but may have an innocent explanation.
- **Interruption** — a timed or state-triggered development that changes the conversation or reveals new truthful information.

Every red herring must have a truthful underlying explanation. Players should be able to trust the evidence system even when they misinterpret what the evidence means.
